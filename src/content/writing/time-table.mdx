Scheduling doesn't kill your fun. For me, it's the reason I can have fun without worrying about anything else.

I have been this guy who schedules life since, I would say, 3rd grade. Back then I used to schedule my homework so I could catch my cartoons on time. They were telecast only once or twice a day (pre-YouTube era, or at least YouTube didn't have these cartoons uploaded yet). I called it my TIME TABLE. Today that same TIME TABLE is helping me schedule my Agents too (the all-glorious world of AI).

## Why a TIME TABLE?

Have you ever had a day where you wake up, have nothing to do, and just feel like sleeping more?

What about the days you have to go to work?

What about the weekend vacation you actually PLANNED?

From my personal experience, when I schedule having fun, I can really have FUN without being worried about anything else. And no, scheduling fun doesn't mean "ok, relax from 2 to 3 PM." It means knowing there's a part of the day where nothing is planned.

## What happened when I stopped

For most of October I didn't schedule anything. I wanted to see the other side of life and check if maybe this scheduling thing was overcomplicated and not that useful.

I liked it for a while. But then things started slipping. I didn't keep track of my car insurance, missed the deadline and had to pay a late fee. My weekends were nothing but scrolling. And at work, even after working hard, I didn't have anything to showcase or present in a nice way, so I couldn't get it over to the stakeholders. Every time something suddenly came up, I wasn't MENTALLY ready for it, and I felt more anxious, not less.

In November I went back to my TIME TABLE habit, and I'm realizing I have more free time to DO NOTHING. Because the important things are already taken care of before they show up later as URGENT things.

## How I do it

This is critical, and I feel this is where most people stop. They either try 50 different apps or try nothing at all and stick to pen and paper. Here's how mine evolved.

**Pen and paper:** In my childhood it was all pen and paper. The low friction and the physical feeling of writing something are unmatched. But this isn't an essay you write once. It's a repeated task that happens every week and every day, so rewriting everything becomes a bottleneck. Still, I recommend it if you're just starting out.

**Apple Notes:** What's easier than writing? Copy-pasting. After buying my first iPhone (THE iPhone 6), I moved to Apple Notes and used its checklist for daily tasks. Simple and easy, but it didn't have the ready-made templates or kanban boards that Notion has.

**Notion:** Ok, my current structure:

- **Section 1:** Top 3 to 5 outcomes for the week
- **Section 2:** Quick Wins (less effort, good ROI)
- **Section 3:** Stuck (waiting or backlogged)
- **Section 4:** The Week, a per-day to-do list like:
  - Mon
    - 9 - 10 AM: Code
    - 10 - 12 PM: Meetings
    - etc.

![My weekly Notion pages since June, and my Monday TIME TABLE](/writing/time-table/notion-weekly.jpg)

Now the part that makes it sustainable: I DUPLICATE this Notion page every week, rename it to the current week, and edit it. Anything I missed or didn't check off last week is still there and moves into this week, which helps me keep track of it. (Ok, AI can schedule things and all, but it cannot know each and every minute of your life or what's on your mind.)

**MOST IMPORTANT STEP:** You need to update it. PERIOD. If this doesn't happen, the week is lost. Same way an AI loses its context, you lose the context of your own life.

## Where AI fits in

Ok, let's zoom out on my life: a 9-to-5 job, a startup hustle, and obviously family and JYMMMMM. My agents are on a TIME TABLE too. For example, I want to buy a Tesla Model Y with 0% APR. So I have an agent with a clear prompt and clear instructions that runs every day at the same time and checks for that deal. Now think: if agents started doing random stuff at random times, what would happen? So why do we think doing random stuff at random times makes us creative? LLMs only became really useful once they had context and clear steps to follow. That's pretty much what my TIME TABLE does for me. I know what to do and when, and in that window I can research, fix or build that work. Don't be an AGENT, but understand that clarity comes from cleaning, decluttering and SCHEDULING. Algorithms have STEPS, and yet they create some of the most random things a human can come up with.

## The downside

I'll be honest. You can become intolerant of people who don't understand the value of this kind of life. And you get addicted to it, to the point where you feel helpless on days you don't have a TIME TABLE.

## Try this

This Sunday, open whatever you have (paper, Notes, Notion) and make the 4 sections: Top 3 to 5 outcomes, Quick Wins, Stuck, and The Week. Fill in only Monday, hour by hour. Leave one block empty on purpose for doing NOTHING. Next Sunday, duplicate it and carry over what you missed. That's it.

## Conclusion

I'm a Muslim and we pray 5 times a day. Here's something to think about: Allah (God) could have asked us to pray in a random way, but He gave us a TIME TABLE for prayers. Maybe He wants us to schedule our days around the times of salah?

And the prayer times aren't hard times like "pray at 5:50 AM and done." They're a range. Say, the 2nd prayer of the day (Zuhr) can be prayed anytime from 12 to 3 PM. There's structure, and there's room.

The gist of this blog was to make myself, and then anybody reading this, understand that a TIME TABLE helps you enjoy life rather than restrict your fun time. And eventually it helps you achieve more, in a fun way, without compromising on your health, faith and family!
